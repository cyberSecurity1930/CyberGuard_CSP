Community Service Project

Cyber Security Awareness Campaign

The Initiative

An initiative by the Computer Science and Engineering (CSE) students of Ramachandra College of Engineering to create a safer digital society through cybersecurity education and awareness.

Why Cyber Awareness Matters

In today's digital world, smartphones, social media, online banking, UPI payments, and internet services have become an essential part of everyday life. However, the growing use of digital technology also brings serious risks, including phishing attacks, fraudulent calls, OTP scams, UPI fraud, identity theft, social media hacking, and malicious links.

Our Cyber Security Awareness Campaign aims to educate students, families, and local communities about these online threats and help them develop safe digital habits.

Through interactive learning, real-life examples, awareness activities, practical demonstrations, and knowledge-based quizzes, we make cybersecurity simple, understandable, and useful for everyone.

Our campaign focuses on helping people identify suspicious activities, protect personal information, secure online accounts, use digital payment services safely, and understand how to respond to cybercrime.

Our mission is simple: Stay Alert. Stay Secure. Stay Cyber Aware.

Explore our learning resources, discover common cyber threats, test your cybersecurity knowledge, and become a responsible digital citizen.

Know the risks

Common Cyber Threats

Recognize these attacks early so you can protect yourself and others.

⚠ High risk
🎣

Phishing

Fake emails, SMS, or sites that trick you into sharing passwords, OTPs, or bank details.

Never click unexpected links. Verify the sender domain. Banks never ask for OTP or full password via email/SMS.
⚠ Critical
🦠

Malware & Ransomware

Malicious software that steals data or locks your files until a ransom is paid.

Keep OS and apps updated. Avoid pirated software. Use reputable antivirus and back up important files offline.
⚠ Common
🔑

Weak Passwords

Reused or simple passwords make account takeover easy for attackers.

Use long unique passphrases. Enable 2FA/MFA everywhere possible. Consider a password manager.
⚠ Financial
💳

UPI & OTP Fraud

Scammers ask you to “receive money” by entering a PIN or share OTPs on calls.

Never share OTP or UPI PIN. You do not need a PIN to receive money. Hang up and call your bank on the official number.
⚠ Social
👤

Social Engineering

Manipulating trust — fake support calls, job offers, or “relative in trouble” stories.

Verify independently. Do not act under pressure. Confirm identity through a known channel before sharing anything.
⚠ Data
📦

Data Breaches

Your credentials may leak from sites you used years ago — then get tried on other accounts.

Change passwords if a service is breached. Never reuse passwords across sites. Monitor account activity.

Quick Protection Tips

  • 1Strong passwords: 12+ characters, mix of words, numbers, symbols. Never reuse.
  • 2OTP & UPI: OTP is only for you. You never need a PIN to receive money.
  • 3Phishing signs: Urgency, poor grammar, mismatched URLs, unexpected attachments.
  • 4Report: Call 1930 (cyber fraud) or visit cybercrime.gov.in. Act within 24 hours for transaction disputes.
Practice

Spot the Phish

Inspect this fictional message. Can you find the red flags?

Inbox — Simulated (not real)

From: security-alert@bankofindia-secure.ru  |  Subject: URGENT: Account locked in 2 hours

Dear Valued Customer,

We detected unusual login from another country. Your account will be permanently locked unless you verify within 2 hours.

Click here immediately:

http://bankofindia-secure.ru/login-verify?user=you

(Example link is non-functional and for learning only.)

🚩 Red flags in this message

  • Suspicious domain (.ru) — not the real bank domain
  • Creates panic with a short deadline
  • Asks you to click a login link from email
  • Generic greeting (“Valued Customer”)
  • HTTP (not HTTPS) style URL pattern

What you should do instead

  • Open the official bank app or type the known website URL yourself
  • Call the number printed on your debit card — not any number in the email
  • Report the message as phishing in your mail client
  • If you already clicked or shared details, change passwords and call 1930
  • Enable transaction alerts on SMS/email for every payment
